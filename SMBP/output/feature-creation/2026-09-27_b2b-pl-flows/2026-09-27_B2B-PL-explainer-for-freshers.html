How the B2B Personal Finance journey works

A fresher's guide to the four finance variants — New Finance, Buyout, Buyout + Top-Up and Debt Consolidation — with the money flows, the journey, and every formula the backend runs. Never worked in lending before? Section 00 is written for you.

00

Start here: the phone-contract analogy

Completely new to lending? Read this box first. Everything else in this page is detail on top of these three ideas.

Buyout = switching phone companies

Debt moves

You have a loan at Bank A. Bank B says: "Come to us — we'll pay off what you owe Bank A, and now you owe us instead, at a cheaper rate." Your debt doesn't disappear. It just moves. You get no cash in your hand.

Rashid: owes Bank A 60,000. Bank B pays Bank A the 60,000. Now Rashid pays Bank B — a smaller monthly amount.

Buyout + Top-Up = switching AND getting a free phone

Debt moves + cash

Same switch, but the new bank lends you more than you owed. The first part pays off the old bank; the extra part is cash for you.

Rashid: Bank B approves 120,000. It sends 60,000 to Bank A and puts the leftover ~59,400 into Rashid's account.

Debt Consolidation = all your bills into one bill

Many → one

You owe money in many places — two credit cards, a loan, an overdraft. Every month you juggle four payments. The bank pays off all of them at once, and now you make one payment — usually smaller in total, because cards charge ~36%/year and a loan charges ~6–11%.

Rashid: was paying 2,500 (loan) + 2,000 (card minimum) = 4,500/mo to two banks. After DC: one payment of ~2,200/mo to one bank.

One picture

Money flows

Notice: in every variant the money for old debts goes bank to bank — it never passes through the customer's hands.

Three things to remember before you read anything else

  1. Settlement money never touches the customer. Our system pays the other bank directly — that's the fraud protection.
  2. Buyout = one old loan. DC = many old debts — and DC is the only variant that includes credit cards.
  3. Cheaper per month ≠ cheaper in total. Stretching 30 months into 48 lowers the instalment but adds total cost — that's why the KFS warning exists.
01

The 30-second big picture

Who is involved, and what problem each variant solves.

SMBP is a marketplace: a channel (a partner like Appro) runs the customer journey, and a bank decides and lends. In the B2B setup there is exactly one bank per channel (AMP-3713). A customer in the UAE often already has loans and credit cards at other banks — so a new personal finance is not always "new money". Sometimes it is really about moving or merging old debt. That is why one journey supports four Transaction Types.

CustomerSalaried or pensioner. Applies in the app, picks a variant, signs T&C + KFS.
Channel (Distribution Portal)Runs identity, fraud, name-screening checks. Owns consents, insurance catalogue, templates.
Bank (Bank Portal — what we design)Owns products, pricing matrix, rule engine, limit assignment, settlement config, offers.
AECB (credit bureau)The single source of truth for the customer's existing loans and cards. Every settlement decision starts from its report.

Regulatory frame (memorise these three numbers): a UAE personal loan is capped at 20× salary, 48 months tenor, and 50% DBR (debt-burden ratio) — CBUAE rules. And CBUAE caps the fee for leaving a bank early at 1% of outstanding, which is the whole reason the buyout market exists.

02

The four finance variants

Same journey, four different money flows. Follow Rashid — salary AED 20,000/mo, one old loan (AED 60,000 outstanding, EMI 2,500) and one credit card (limit 40,000, balance 30,000).

New Finance

Fresh

A brand-new loan. Nothing is settled; old cards and loans stay untouched. The whole approved amount lands in the customer's account (minus fees).

Rashid: borrows 100,000 for a wedding. His old loan and card stay as they are. His DBR must still fit: new EMI + old obligations ≤ 50% of income.
Settlement screenSkipped entirely
Cash to customerFull approved amount
AvailabilityAlways shown, can't be disabled
Offer text"You're approved for"

Buyout

Settlement · 1 contract

The new bank pays off one existing personal finance at another bank. The customer's debt simply moves to a (usually cheaper) lender. Top-up = 0.

Rashid: his old loan costs him 2,500/mo with 30 months left. New bank settles the 60,000 (+ 1% exit fee, absorbed by the bank) and re-lends over 48 months at 5.49% → new EMI ≈ 1,409. He pays 1,091 less each month — but for longer, so total cost can be higher. That trade-off is a mandatory KFS warning.
Settlement screenSingle-select (radio) · credit cards hidden
Eligible contractsUnsecured instalments only
Availability≥1 in-scope instalment + buyout_enabled
Key docLiability/settlement letter from old bank

Buyout + Top-Up

Settlement + cash

A buyout plus extra cash on top of the settlement — one loan, one instalment. The extra cash is capped by Max Top Up % of the approved facility (pricing matrix config).

Rashid: approved 120,000. Settlement takes 60,600 (60,000 + absorbed exit fee 600). Top-up cash to his account = 59,400 — allowed, because it is under the product's 50% top-up cap (60,000). The bank may require him to declare the top-up purpose (config toggle).
Settlement screenSame as Buyout: single-select, CC hidden
Cash to customerApproved − settlement, ≤ Max Top Up %
Availability≥1 in-scope instalment + buyout_topup_enabled
Extra configTop-up % cap · purpose-required toggle

Debt Consolidation

Multi-settlement

Settles multiple liabilities — credit cards, unsecured loans, overdrafts — into one instalment. The pitch: replace ~36% p.a. revolving card debt with a ~5–11% reducing-rate loan. That rate difference is the "Save AED X/mo" banner.

Rashid: settles his loan (60,000) and card (30,000). Pool = 90,000 × 1.05 buffer = 94,500 → one EMI ≈ 2,197. Before, he paid 2,500 + ~2,000 card minimum = 4,500. Saving ≈ 2,303/mo.

Extra gateHard gates G1–G5 must pass (§05)

Offer text"Pre-approved" + savings banner

06

The maths, with Rashid's numbers

Six formulas cover 90% of the backend. P = principal, R = annual rate, N = months.
F1 · EMI (monthly instalment) EMI = P × (R/12) × (1+R/12)^N / ((1+R/12)^N − 1) AMP-11 · the standard amortisation formula — everything else builds on it
F2 · DBR room (how much monthly payment fits) DBR Room = MaxDBR% × Finalized Income − existing monthly obligations AMP-680 · Max DBR = 50% (CBUAE); obligations counted per F5
F3 · Max eligibility (biggest loan the room can carry) Max Eligibility = PV of DBR Room = DBR Room × (1 − (1+Base/12)^(−N)) / (Base/12) AMP-2239 · then capped by product min/max and decision boundary
F5 · Monthly obligation per bureau contract Credit card / OD : MAX(cfg% × CreditLimit, cfg% × Balance) Instalment loan : PaymentAmount (as reported) AMP-3732 AC4 · cfg% comes from the AECB Contract Scope config
F6 · Total buyout amount Total = Settlement + Absorbed Exit Fee + Top-Up Absorbed Exit Fee = min(1% × outstanding, old-bank fee) × Absorption% AMP-3721 IA1 · 1% cap per CBUAE Reg. 29/2011; Absorption% per pricing scheme
F11 · DC new EMI and post-consolidation DBR New EMI = PMT(MinRate/12, MinTenure, Σ in-scope balances × 1.05) Post-DBR = (New EMI + out-of-scope obligations) / income → must pass G5 AMP-2328 / AMP-2329 · the 1.05 is a 5% settlement buffer
F13 · Net amount credited (what the customer actually receives) Net = Approved − Processing Fee − Insurance Premium − Settlement Amount AMP-3404 AC9 · insurance is % × amount or fixed AED, always "reduce from finance" in B2B

Worked end-to-end: Rashid takes Buyout + Top-Up

  1. Obligations (F5): card 5% × 40,000 = 2,000 + loan EMI 2,500 = 4,500/mo
  2. Room (F2): 50% × 20,000 − 4,500 = 5,500/mo
  3. Eligibility (F3): at 6% base, 48m → 5,500 × 42.58 ≈ 234,000 → bank approves 120,000 (product cap)
  4. Buyout (F6): 60,000 + (1% × 60,000 × 100% absorbed = 600) + top-up = total; top-up cash = 120,000 − 60,600 = 59,400 ✓ under the 50% top-up cap (60,000)
  5. New EMI (F1): 120,000 @ 5.49% / 48m ≈ 2,790/mo — old loan's 2,500 disappears, so his total monthly barely moves while he pockets 59,400
  6. Net credited (F13): 120,000 − fee 1,260 − insurance 600 − settlement 60,600 = 57,540 to Rashid's account
Compliance guard-rail: no story asserts the 48-month CBUAE tenor cap — the generic tenor formula allows up to 300 months and only Product Setup's Max Tenor protects us. We recommended an explicit platform validation for PL.
07

Glossary — the words you'll hear in standup

Transaction Type — the variant, set once per product. Drives screens, pricing lookup and (planned) rule-engine strategies.
AECB — Al Etihad Credit Bureau. The report listing every loan/card the customer holds anywhere in the UAE.
DBR — Debt Burden Ratio: monthly obligations ÷ income. Regulatory ceiling 50%.
Settlement / Liability letter — old bank's letter stating the exact payoff amount. Valid 1–365 days (config); expired letters are flagged.
FSTL — First Salary Transfer Letter, a Yes/No pricing factor: does this scheme require proof the salary moves to the new bank?
Absorption % — how much of the old bank's 1% exit fee the new bank eats (0–100%, per scheme).
In-scope contract — a bureau contract that passes all filters and can be settled. Everything else shows an EXCLUDED badge (or is hidden).
Sales Lead — new status for manually-handled applicants (self-employed, extra income, low income). No auto-decision.
Cooling-off — 5 business days to cancel after signing (CBUAE). Waivable; our default checkbox = waived.
Grace period — days before the first instalment. Journey range = min of banks' minimums to max of maximums (fallback 30–99).
KFS — Key Facts Statement: the two-page regulatory summary of rate, fees and warnings the customer must acknowledge.
Shortfall fallback — buyout ask > approved amount → system pivots the application to Debt Consolidation, with re-acknowledged T&C/KFS.
