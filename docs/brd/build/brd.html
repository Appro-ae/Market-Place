APPENDIX A

How Consumer Credit Score 3.0 works

The goal is unchanged — rank customers by the likelihood of reaching 90+ days past due on at least one contract within 12 months. What changes is how the score is built: one scored population becomes two, each with its own scorecards, scale and range codes. On both scales, a higher score means lower risk.

Population A

Mature Credit · 300 – 850

  • Oldest credit contract is older than 3 months
  • 7 segment scorecards
  • Range codes M0 – M9 across five risk groups, Very High to Very Low
  • A score of 700 means 20 : 1 odds — a 4.8% expected bad rate. The odds double every 40 points.
Population B

New to Credit · 300 – 650

  • No valid credit history, or a first contract opened in the last 3 months, with telco or account data in the last 12 months
  • 3 segment scorecards built on telco, cheque, income and account data
  • Range codes N0 – N9 across four risk groups — no Very Low Risk band
  • A score of 475 means 7 : 1 odds — a 12.0% expected bad rate. The odds double every 25 points.
